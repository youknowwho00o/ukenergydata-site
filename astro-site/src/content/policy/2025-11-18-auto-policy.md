**UK Energy Policy Update – 18 November 2025**

--- 

### 1. Government Energy Policy Announcements

On 17 November 2025, the UK Government released its latest comprehensive energy policy update emphasizing its commitment to net zero by 2050, with accelerated interim targets for 2030. Key highlights include:

- **Increased Allocation for Low Carbon Energy**: The governmenßt announced a £3.5 billion enhancement to the Contracts for Difference (CfD) scheme, targeting further offshore wind capacity expansions and new small modular reactor (SMR) developments.
- **Hydrogen Strategy Advancement**: Funding of £500 million was allocated to develop large-scale green hydrogen production facilities, aiming to integrate hydrogen into industrial and heating sectors.
- **Energy Efficiency and Retrofit Focus**: Expansion of the Energy Company Obligation (ECO) scheme to support more households in installing insulation, low-carbon heating, and ventilation systems. The government also set an ambition to phase out gas boilers in newly constructed homes by 2030.
- **Smart Grid and Storage Investment**: New initiatives support grid flexibility, including incentives for battery storage and demand response technologies.

---

### 2. Updates on Subsidies and Schemes

#### Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS)
- The BUS continues with a renewed funding tranche of £150 million for FY 2025-26, focusing primarily on heat pump installations.
- Eligibility criteria have been widened to include more social housing providers and low-income households.
- Average grant values have slightly increased to incentivize the adoption of higher efficiency heat pump models.

#### Contracts for Difference (CfD)
- The additional £3.5 billion announced will focus on further rounds (AR7) of CfD auctions scheduled for early 2026.
- Future CfD allocation rounds will place greater emphasis on emerging technologies such as tidal energy and advanced nuclear.
- Strike prices show minor adjustments reflecting market conditions but remain highly competitive against fossil fuel generation costs.

#### Energy Company Obligation (ECO)
- The ECO4 scheme has been extended through 2027 with a budget uplift of £600 million.
- New measures target improved support for hard-to-treat homes, and increased funding for low-carbon heating upgrades.
- Enhanced data-sharing obligations require suppliers to better identify eligible households.

---

### 3. Ofgem Regulatory Updates

Ofgem has introduced several regulatory updates in Q4 2025:

- **Price Cap Review**: Confirmed maintenance of the energy price cap for regulated tariffs through Spring 2026, balancing consumer protection with supplier viability amid volatile wholesale prices.
- **Smart Meter Rollout Acceleration**: Regulatory changes mandate that all new homes must be fitted with smart meters starting 1 January 2026. Deadline for full national rollout moved forward to 2027.
- **Grid Access Reforms**: New policies aim to reduce delays and costs associated with connecting renewable energy projects to the distribution network.
- **Consumer Protection Enhancements**: Strengthened protections against disconnection during winter months and increased support frameworks for vulnerable customers.
- **Decarbonisation Incentives for Suppliers**: Ofgem published a consultation on introducing carbon intensity benchmarks into supplier performance metrics.

---

### 4. Expected Impacts on Businesses and Households

- **Businesses**: 
  - Renewable energy developers benefit from expanded CfD budgets and streamlined grid access, likely accelerating new project developments.
  - Industrial sectors involved in hydrogen and battery storage technologies will gain from direct government investments.
  - Energy-intensive manufacturing may face increased compliance costs aligned with new carbon intensity regulation enhancements.

- **Households**:
  - Increased access to heat pump subsidies via the Boiler Upgrade Scheme will help reduce reliance on fossil fuel heating, lowering long-term energy bills.
  - Enhanced ECO funding will enable more low-income households to access insulation and efficient heating, mitigating fuel poverty risks.
  - Stability in energy price caps provides short-term financial relief but may be offset by increased energy efficiency compliance costs in the medium term.
  - The requirement for smart meters in new builds promotes more active energy management and potential savings.

---

**Summary**

The UK government’s November 2025 energy policy update reinforces an ambitious low-carbon transition agenda, balancing investment in clean energy infrastructure with consumer protection measures. Enhanced subsidies and regulatory reforms from both government and Ofgem promote increased renewable deployment, energy efficiency, and system flexibility. These actions will collectively influence market dynamics, facilitating decarbonisation goals while supporting vulnerable populations.
